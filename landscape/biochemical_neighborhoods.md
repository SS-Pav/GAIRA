# Emergent Biochemical Neighborhoods

## Major Axes Identified

### 1. Aromatic Amino Acid Hub
**Defining motifs**: phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan
**Co-occurrence**: J=0.56-0.63 (strongest cluster in the corpus)
**Conditions**: present across nearly all disease conditions
**Assessment**: **Biology-driven** — aromatic amino acids are the dominant SERS features in serum. Their ubiquity is real biochemistry, not methodology bias.

### 2. Protein / Amide Backbone
**Defining motifs**: protein, collagen, amide I, proline
**Co-occurrence**: protein-amide I J=0.41, protein-collagen J=0.35
**Conditions**: all serum/tissue conditions
**Assessment**: **Biology-driven** — amide I/II/III bands are fundamental protein markers

### 3. Lipid / Membrane
**Defining motifs**: lipid, cholesterol, phospholipid themes
**Co-occurrence**: protein-lipid J=0.48 (high — these co-occur in most serum studies)
**Conditions**: broad, slightly liver-enriched
**Assessment**: **Mixed** — lipid signal is real but its ubiquity makes it a weak differentiator

### 4. Purine / Nucleotide
**Defining motifs**: adenine, guanine, AMP
**Co-occurrence**: adenine-guanine J=0.25 (moderate, base-specific)
**Conditions**: cancer_stem_cell, HCC, liver_cancer, bacterial_identification
**Assessment**: **Biology-driven** — purine metabolism changes are expected in cancer and infection

### 5. Glycan / Carbohydrate
**Defining motifs**: glucose, galactose, mannose, glycogen
**Conditions**: HCC (glycan alterations), fibrosis (glycosaminoglycans)
**Assessment**: **Biology-driven but sparse** — only 6 motifs, fewer sources

### 6. Serum Baseline Cluster
**Defining motifs**: broad overlap of phenylalanine + protein + lipid
**Conditions**: healthy_control, reference_method
**Assessment**: **Methodology-driven** — this is what "normal serum SERS" looks like. It sets the baseline that disease signals must deviate from.

## Key Observation
The largest biochemical axis is the aromatic amino acid hub (Phe/Tyr/Trp). This is not a discovery — it reflects well-known SERS chemistry. The more interesting signals are the condition-specific enrichments within purine, glycan, and redox components, which are sparser but potentially more discriminative.
